Getting started

Logging a meal

Tap + to open the scanner. Point it at a barcode or a nutrition label (or, on iPhones that can scan a plate of food, at your plate), or choose a photo from your library. Check the foods and amounts, change anything that looks off, then log it.

Water

Add water on Home adds a glass of 250 ml, and − takes the last one away.

Widgets

On your Home Screen, touch and hold an empty spot, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and choose Meals. On your Lock Screen, touch and hold it, tap Customize, choose the Lock Screen and add Meals.

Questions

Which iPhones can scan a plate of food?

Plate scanning uses Apple Intelligence, so it needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later with iOS 27 and Apple Intelligence turned on. Barcodes and nutrition labels work on every iPhone that runs iOS 26 or later.

How accurate are the estimates?

A photo estimate is a good starting point, not a kitchen scale. Where Meals offers its closer look and you're online, it takes a closer look at plate photos using Apple's Private Cloud Compute and corrects the estimate if needed. Portion sizes and hidden oil or butter are still the hardest things to see in a photo, so check the amounts and change anything that looks wrong. Every amount can be edited, before and after logging.

What does the nutrition grade mean?

Each meal gets a grade from A (best) to E, based on its sugar, saturated fat, salt, fibre and protein, and how much fruit, vegetables and legumes it contains. "Estimated" means Meals worked the grade out itself, from a photo, a label or a product's nutrition values. "No grade" means there wasn't enough information. The day grade combines the day's meals, weighted by their calories.

How is my daily target worked out?

From your height, weight, age, sex and activity level, adjusted for the pace you chose, and never below a safe minimum of 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 kcal for men. With Apple Health connected, it rises on days you move more than usual. You can change your details at any time in Profile.

How do I connect or disconnect Apple Health?

Meals asks during setup. To change what Meals can read or write later, open the Health app, tap your profile, then Apps and Meals.

Is my data private?

Yes. Your food diary stays on your iPhone, there's no account, and nothing is tracked. At most two things leave your iPhone: a barcode number when you scan a product, and, where Meals offers its closer look, a plate photo, which Apple's Private Cloud Compute checks and then deletes. Neither ever reaches me. The Privacy Policy has the details.

How do I delete my data?

Delete entries in the app, or delete the app to remove everything from your iPhone. Data already saved to Apple Health stays there until you delete it in the Health app.

I have a new iPhone. Do I need to pay again?

No. Meals is a one-time purchase, so download it again from the App Store with the same Apple Account. Your entries come with your iPhone backup or transfer.

A note on health

Meals gives estimates for everyday nutrition tracking. It isn't a medical device and doesn't give medical advice. If you're pregnant, have a medical condition or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or dietitian before following calorie targets.
